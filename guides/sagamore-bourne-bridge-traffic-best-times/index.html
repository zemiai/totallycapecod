Sagamore and Bourne Bridge Traffic: Peak Times and When to Cross

When Sagamore and Bourne bridge traffic peaks, per MassDOT traffic studies and holiday advisories, plus construction news and how to check live delays.

By Versel JohnsonPublished November 13, 2026How we fact-check

The Sagamore and Bourne bridges are the only roadway connections between Cape Cod and the mainland [2], so when they back up, every driver on or off the Cape feels it. This guide pulls together what MassDOT's traffic studies and travel advisories say about when the bridges are busiest, what's happening with construction and the replacement project, and how to check delays before you go.

For the current picture, open our live Sagamore and Bourne bridge traffic page.

The two bridges at a glance

The Sagamore and Bourne bridges provide the only roadway connections between Cape Cod and the mainland [2]. Both are owned, operated and maintained by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers [1].

Sagamore Bridge Bourne Bridge
Road it carries U.S. Route 6 [2] State Route 28 [2]
Length 1,408 feet [2] 2,384 feet [2]
Travel lanes Two in each direction [1] Two in each direction [1]
Average annual daily traffic, 2019 61,990 vehicles [6] 46,500 vehicles [6]

Both bridges opened to traffic in June 1935 [2]. Each is 48 feet, 8 inches wide, including four 10-foot travel lanes [2].

When bridge traffic is heaviest

Summer, and especially summer weekends

MassDOT's Cape Cod Bridges environmental impact statement says traffic volumes and congestion levels near the canal are typically highest during the summer [6], and the state's bridge program page says traffic delays are prevalent during the summer with frequent backups [1].

MassDOT's earlier Cape Cod Canal Transportation Study counted traffic in August and October 2014 [4]:

Bridge Summer daily traffic (2014) Non-summer daily traffic (2014)
Sagamore 65,900 [4] 41,400 [4]
Bourne 56,500 [4] 38,000 [4]

That study found daily volumes on the Bourne and Sagamore were 49% and 59% higher in summer than in non-summer periods [4]. It also found February traffic is only 54% (Bourne) to 60% (Sagamore) of August volumes [4].

Summer Saturdays at midday

MassDOT's traffic engineering report for the bridge project says the summer Saturday midday is the peak period for total canal crossings in both directions, counting on-Cape and off-Cape traffic [7]. The canal study explains why: summer Saturday is a period of high traffic demand because vacation rentals on the Cape generally begin and end on Saturday [4].

In its 2014 counts, the canal study measured a summer Saturday period of 10 AM to 12 PM [4]. Its summer Saturday peak-hour count was 5,800 vehicles on the Sagamore and 4,750 on the Bourne; the Bourne's summer weekday afternoon peak hour was also 4,750 [4].

Summer Sundays and weekday rush hours

The canal study says the location and substandard design of the Route 6 interchange it calls Exit 1C contribute to congestion on Route 6 westbound, particularly during summer Sundays [5]. The study's 2014 counts also covered summer weekday peaks of 7 to 9 AM and 4 to 6 PM [4].

For the bridge project, MassDOT chose the fall weekday afternoon peak as its design period [7]. In that peak hour, the Sagamore's steep grade, narrow lanes and the lane reduction on Route 3 cause queues to extend about 2 miles from the bridge, and queues on northbound Route 28 often extend about 2 miles [6]. The Bourne Rotary is a frequent source of congestion during peak travel hours [6].

Spring and fall shoulder seasons

MassDOT's canal study noted that congestion, historically a summer problem, now often occurs during the spring and fall shoulder seasons [5]. More recently, the bridge project's traffic report found 2024 counts in line with its 2019 base-year volumes [7].

What the numbers suggest about timing your trip

None of the MassDOT studies or advisories we reviewed names a single "best time" to cross. Here's what the data does show:

  • Season makes a big difference. In MassDOT's 2014 counts, October daily traffic was 41,400 vehicles on the Sagamore and 38,000 on the Bourne, versus 65,900 and 56,500 in August [4]. MassDOT says that 85% of the year, traffic in its study area is at or below fall weekday afternoon peak levels [6].
  • Summer Saturday midday is the peak period for total canal crossings in both directions, according to MassDOT's traffic report [7]; the 2014 canal study measured the Saturday peak hour within 10 AM to 12 PM [4].
  • On weekdays, the 2014 canal study measured peaks of 7 to 9 AM and 4 to 6 PM [4], and the bridge project uses the fall weekday afternoon peak as its design period [7].
  • Holiday weekends follow their own pattern; see below.

Holiday weekends: what MassDOT forecast in 2025 and 2026

MassDOT issues a statewide travel advisory before major holidays. These forecasts cover Massachusetts highways generally, not just the bridges, but the 2026 Fourth of July advisory called out Cape Cod by name [10].

  • Thanksgiving 2025: statewide, MassDOT forecast increased traffic throughout the week, with the heaviest traffic Monday through Wednesday; it expected heavier traffic than usual on Monday, November 24, and Tuesday, November 25, with added delays during midday hours on Wednesday, November 26, Thanksgiving Day and throughout the weekend after [8]. It expected heavy volumes on Sunday, November 30, and encouraged drivers to travel during morning hours if possible [8].
  • Memorial Day 2026: Friday, May 22, was expected to be the busiest travel day [9]. On Saturday, drivers were encouraged to avoid late-morning and early-afternoon travel, with traffic lightest in the evening; on Sunday and Monday, traffic was expected to be heaviest at midday [9].
  • Fourth of July 2026: the heaviest traffic was expected on Thursday, July 2, and Friday, July 3 [10]. MassDOT expected increased congestion on routes into Cape Cod on Saturday, July 4, and heavier traffic in the morning and midday on Sunday, July 5, from travelers leaving Cape Cod [10].
  • Labor Day 2026: Friday, September 4, was expected to see heavy traffic all day, with major delays from 10 AM to 6 PM [11]. Saturday and Monday were expected to be lighter, with major delays limited to 10 AM to 2 PM; Sunday was expected to be heavier from the morning into the evening [11].

Construction and lane closures

The Army Corps posts Cape Cod Canal bridge alerts, linked from the Cape Cod Commission's traffic page [15]. Check them before a trip.

Foundation load testing near the Sagamore Bridge. MassDOT is testing foundations at four locations near the Sagamore Bridge, two north of the canal and two south [12]. The work is intermittent and limited to daytime hours on weekdays, starting south of the bridge near the former Christmas Tree Shops location [12].

The Sagamore Bridge replacement: where it stands

The Cape Cod Bridges Project covers replacing both the Sagamore Bridge and the Bourne Bridge [3]. As of September 28, 2026, the state said the Sagamore Bridge replacement was moving into its final procurement phase [13]; the Army Corps owns the Bourne Bridge and will remain responsible for maintaining it until it is replaced in a later phase [14].

Milestones so far in 2026:

  • June 26, 2026: the Federal Highway Administration signed the combined Final Environmental Impact Statement and Record of Decision for the project [3].
  • July 23, 2026: MassDOT held a design public hearing for the Sagamore Bridge replacement [12].
  • September 24, 2026: MassDOT issued a Request for Proposals to three shortlisted design-build teams [14][12].
  • September 28, 2026: the state announced authorization of $956 million in federal grant funding [13].

What's next. MassDOT anticipates a Notice to Proceed in late 2027, with construction expected to begin in winter 2027 [13]; its hearing FAQ puts the start of major construction in spring 2028 [14]. MassDOT expects the new eastbound span to open in 2033 [13]. MassDOT and the Army Corps have jointly applied for more than $1 billion in federal funding to replace the Bourne Bridge [13].

Will traffic be cut during construction? MassDOT plans to maintain two lanes of traffic in each direction throughout construction [13], and says it will keep at least two travel lanes open in each direction across the canal [14].

How to check bridge traffic before you go

  • Our live page: Cape Cod bridge traffic right now shows current delays on both bridges.
  • Mass511: MassDOT's Mass511 app and www.mass511.com show live cameras, travel times, real-time traffic conditions and project information; you can subscribe to text and email alerts, or dial 511 and select a route to hear real-time conditions [9][11].
  • GoTime: the Cape Cod Commission lists MassDOT's GoTime app, which provides real-time travel time information [15].
  • Army Corps bridge alerts: the Cape Cod Commission's traffic page links to the Corps' Cape Cod Canal bridge alerts [15].

Quick answers

When is Sagamore Bridge traffic the worst?

MassDOT's traffic studies say traffic volumes and congestion near the Cape Cod Canal are typically highest in the summer [6], and its bridge project's traffic report identifies the summer Saturday midday as the peak period for total canal crossings in both directions [7]. Summer Saturdays are busy because vacation rentals on the Cape generally begin and end on Saturday [4].

What is the best time to cross the Cape Cod bridges?

None of the MassDOT studies or advisories we reviewed names a single best time to cross. MassDOT's 2014 counts show both bridges carry far less traffic outside summer: in an October 2014 count, about 41,400 vehicles a day on the Sagamore and 38,000 on the Bourne, versus 65,900 and 56,500 in August [4]. MassDOT also says that 85% of the year, traffic is at or below the level of the fall weekday afternoon peak period [6]. Check live conditions before you leave [15].

How many cars cross the Sagamore Bridge each day?

In 2019 the Sagamore Bridge averaged 61,990 vehicles a day and the Bourne Bridge 46,500, according to MassDOT's Cape Cod Bridges environmental impact statement [6].

Who owns the Sagamore and Bourne bridges?

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers owns, operates and maintains both bridges [1]. MassDOT is leading the project to replace them, starting with the Sagamore Bridge [13][14].

Will the Sagamore Bridge close during the replacement?

MassDOT says it plans to maintain two lanes of traffic in each direction throughout construction [13], and its hearing FAQ says at least two travel lanes will stay open in each direction across the canal [14].

When does construction on the new Sagamore Bridge start?

MassDOT's September 28, 2026, release says a Notice to Proceed is anticipated in late 2027, with construction expected to begin in winter 2027 [13]; its public hearing FAQ says major construction is expected to begin in spring 2028 [14]. MassDOT expects the new eastbound span to open in 2033 [13].

How do I check Cape Cod bridge traffic right now?

MassDOT's Mass511 website and app show live cameras, travel times and real-time traffic conditions, and you can dial 511 and select a route to hear real-time conditions [9][11]. The Cape Cod Commission also points to MassDOT's GoTime app and the Army Corps' Cape Cod Canal bridge alerts [15].
